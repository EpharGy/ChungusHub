# Stats screen: architecture & maintenance

Everything a reader has written here, counted in one pass and drawn as one screen, plus the deck of shareable cards that screen can hand them. It is a **read-only surface over data every other subsystem already owns**: nothing here writes a row, nothing here is stored, and no figure on it exists anywhere but in the answer to one call.

## Two claims run through every number, and the screen says both out loud

**A chat is a tree, so "how many" has two answers.** `effort` counts the whole message forest: every reroll, every abandoned fork, every alternate greeting. `story` counts only the branch each chat is open at, which is what a reader would actually read. Both ship, because neither is the honest answer to both questions: a reroll you threw away is still a reply you asked for and paid for, and it is still not part of the story. The panel labels them as separate things and never presents one as the other. `shape.abandoned` is the difference, counted from the other side (`effort.messages - story.messages`), so the two can be checked against each other.

**Nothing is estimated.** A turn with no token count is skipped by the token totals rather than priced from its text, and **every measured total ships the number of turns it covers**. Those denominators are not decoration: they differ from each other by design (a turn can carry a token count and no timing, or a timing and no reasoning), and an imported SillyTavern chat typically carries timings and no prompt tokens at all, since that format never recorded them (architecture/sillytavern-interchange.md). A screen that showed an average without its denominator would present a corner of the library as the whole of it, and on a library built from imports that corner is small. **Every figure also names itself**: one row can carry a total and an average at once, so each says which it is rather than leaving a bare number to be read as whichever the reader assumed. `measured.assistantTurns` is what lets the panel say what share of replies is measured at all, and the "What it cost" block is deliberately last and deliberately fenced for the same reason. Nothing may add a figure here that the app did not measure, including a token count re-derived from message text with the app's own tokenizer: writing an estimate beside a measurement makes both unreadable.

## The aggregate

[`getUserStats`](../server/db.ts) lives in db.ts beside the chat-list aggregates, for the same reason they do: it reads **every message body in the database** and must turn them into numbers on the server, never ship them over the bridge. It is the most expensive read the app has.

**One streamed pass does the bulk of it.** `selectIter` (the one caller of it) walks `messages` a row at a time rather than materialising the table, and folds each row into the effort totals, the story totals, the activity buckets, the per-character tallies, the shape counts, the record holders and the measured spend at the same time. A second pass would be a second read of every body. Everything that is not per-message (shelf counts, lorebook entries) is a separate small query around it.

Two lookups are resolved before the pass so it never joins: `activePathRows()` gives the set of on-branch message ids (and, counted per chat, the longest story), and one query maps each chat to its character. `countWords` is **whitespace-separated runs and nothing cleverer**, and it is the only word rule in the app: roleplay prose is asterisks, quotes and two languages at once, so any rule that stripped punctuation or split on letters would answer differently per language while claiming to be the same figure.

The one parse that can fail is a lorebook's JSON payload, which is caught per book: a book already broken everywhere else must not take the whole screen down with it.

### The activity series is zone-free on purpose

The pass emits `[bucketStart, count]` pairs at **fifteen minute** granularity and takes no view on where a day starts. That number is load-bearing: every real-world UTC offset is a multiple of fifteen minutes, so a client folding these into its own local days never has to split a bucket. Widen it and a reader on a half-hour offset gets their late-night messages filed under the wrong date, their streak broken at a midnight that never happened for them, and a "busiest day" one day off the one they remember. Bucketing server-side by UTC day would bake that error in for everyone outside UTC; shipping one timestamp per message to avoid it would ship the table.

**Days therefore belong to the client**, and [`src/lib/stats/derive.ts`](../src/lib/stats/derive.ts) is where they are made: only the browser knows the reader's zone and its daylight-saving history. Its day stepping goes through the `Date` constructor rather than adding 24 hours, so the one day a year that is 23 or 25 hours long still steps exactly one day and a streak survives the clock change. `currentStreak` deliberately holds while today is still empty: a streak is not broken until a whole day passes with nothing in it, so someone opening the panel at breakfast is not told their run ended overnight. `longestStreak` and `busiestDay` both keep the **earliest** of a tie, since a record is when it was first set. `primeHours` returns the narrowest wrap-around window holding half of everything, rather than a single peak hour, which is noise on a small library and says nothing on a large one.

## The screen

[`StatsView.svelte`](../src/lib/components/stats/StatsView.svelte) is a chat-area overlay (`uiStore` overlay id `stats`), with **exactly two doors and neither is the TitleBar**: the welcome landing's action row and the composer's menu. The TitleBar's centred cluster is the open chat's own tools, and this screen is about the whole library, so it does not belong there (architecture/ui-shell-settings.md).

[`statsStore`](../src/lib/stores/stats.svelte.ts) is the only caller of the aggregate. **Nothing may call it on a render, on a sync hint, or anywhere in `boot()`**: the panel asks when it opens and pays for the pass once. The figures are a snapshot of that moment and are deliberately not re-derived when a message lands, because a headline number that twitches while it is being read is one nobody trusts, and because the answer costs a full pass. The panel states when the count was taken and offers a refresh. A failure stays **on the panel** rather than going to a toast: this surface has nothing else to show, so a silent empty state would read as an empty library.

Section order is the order a reader cares in: the one big number, the calendar, the people, the habit, the shape, the receipts. **Nothing above the heatmap changes height with the data**, so the picture people screenshot sits in the same place on every library. The body reads down **one measured column** (`.measure`, capped and centered): the overlay is as wide as the chat column, and a stat row stretched across a reading measure separates its figure from its label. The hero figure is set in the story face (`--font-body`): the number is the book the reader wrote, so it wears the type their story does. The door to the pictures is a **labelled button**, not a bare icon, and it is disabled while the library is empty, since a deck of zeros is not a picture anyone posts.

**Every row in the three lists carries an `InfoTip`, without exception.** A row is a bare label and a bare figure, and which of them need explaining is not a judgement a reader can make from the outside: an ⓘ on some rows and not others reads as "the ones without it are obvious", which is exactly the wrong signal on the rows that quietly are not. A new row ships its tip, and the tip says what the label cannot rather than repeating it. What a chart leaves behind is said as **facts, not prose**: the figures under the heatmap and the clock are value-plus-caption chips, because a number the reader has to parse out of a sentence is a number they will misread. Each caption names its figure fully, which is why the chips carry no tips. The token rows in "What it cost" carry a **per-turn average beside the total** (computed over the same measured turns as the total, so the honesty rule below holds), because a raw token total in the millions answers no question a reader has.

Three pieces carry their own rules. The **heatmap** ([`StatsHeatmap.svelte`](../src/lib/components/stats/StatsHeatmap.svelte)) holds **every week the reader has written** and opens scrolled to the present: the panel is only ever so wide, so it shows the most recent stretch that fits and scrolls back through the rest, rather than cutting off the years before last. It still never shows more calendar than the reader has lived, starting at their first active week, so a library three weeks old is three weeks wide instead of a year of grey. Years sit in **a row of their own above the months**, each ruled across the weeks it covers: a year folded into the month labels lands four weeks from its neighbour and prints straight on top of it, and a grid scrolled back four years with no year anywhere is a row of bare month names. The day-name gutter sits **outside** the scroller: inside it, a grid that opens at the present would start with the gutter already off the left edge. The colour scale is relative to the **ninetieth percentile day, not the busiest one**, or a single all-nighter flattens every ordinary day to the palest step. Days that have not happened yet are drawn transparent rather than as quiet ones, and today wears a ring, since it is the square the "opens at the present" claim is anchored to. The **cast** ([`StatsCast.svelte`](../src/lib/components/stats/StatsCast.svelte)) drops a character the library no longer holds instead of drawing a blank row: the count is real, but with nothing to name or picture it the row would be an unlabelled bar, and the panel states the full total separately so nothing goes missing silently. Rows are **numbered and dated** ("since Mar 2024", off `firstAt`): the list is sorted, and "sorted by what" is not something a reader should have to infer from bar widths. The **book comparison** ([`format.ts`](../src/lib/stats/format.ts)) is a ladder where each entry is roughly twice the one below, so a weekend's writing and four years of it both land on a book the reader has heard of; the copy says "about as long as" because published word counts differ by edition and translation.

`format.ts` also holds the rule that keeps an unmeasured library honest at the last step: `average` and `share` return **null** rather than zero when there is nothing to divide by, so no template can print a measured-looking `0.0s`.

## The pictures

[`poster.ts`](../src/lib/stats/poster.ts) draws a **deck of 1080 by 1920 cards** on a canvas, one card at a time. **Drawn, never screenshotted**: rasterising the DOM would mean a dependency the app does not have, and it would inherit the panel's scrolling, its glass and its exact width, none of which belong in a picture meant to be looked at on a phone.

Portrait, because every place a card lands is 9:16 and a square arrives letterboxed on all of them. **Four cards at most, and every one ships full**: what you wrote (the word count, the book it matches, four tiles, and the split bar of whose words the page is), your cast (the lead at a size worth looking at, the runners-up ranked against them), when you wrote (the days, the calendar, the runs, the shape of a day), and the records (a lead superlative, four in a grid, the first-words date anchoring the foot). `posterCards` **drops a card the snapshot cannot fill** rather than drawing a heading over empty space, which reads as a picture that failed to load instead of one with room to breathe: the cast card needs a counted character the library can still name (which is why `posterCards` takes the resolved names, not just the snapshot), the time card needs an active day, the records card needs five superlatives and the date. A cast of one still fills its page by drawing the figures between the two of them. The drop rules are pinned in [`poster.test.ts`](../src/lib/stats/poster.test.ts), because a card silently missing looks like design. The split bar's right side says **"everyone else", not "your cast"**: the remainder holds system turns too, and the two sides of one bar must sum to the total it is drawn against.

Every card is built on one scaffold, so the deck reads as a set: the hairline frame (the card keeps its own edge posted onto a background its own colour), a main glow and a fainter echo in card-specific corners, the eyebrow with its accent rule, and the wordmark foot with the count's date. The big figures are set in the reader's **story face** (`--font-body`) with the UI face around them, the same two voices the app itself speaks in.

It wears the reader's own theme, resolved off the live document through a **probe element** rather than `getPropertyValue`, because the `--color-*` names are aliases onto `--theme-*` and reading them raw returns the literal `var(…)` text (architecture/ui-shell-settings.md). Before drawing it explicitly `document.fonts.load`s **every weight it sets**, then awaits `fonts.ready`: `ready` alone only covers faces something on screen already asked for, and canvas silently substitutes a system face for the rest, so the picture would come out in the wrong type with no error anywhere. Canvas has no `color-mix`, so the heatmap's four steps are mixed by hand from the two resolved colours.

**The preview IS the picture**: [`StatsPosterDialog.svelte`](../src/lib/components/stats/StatsPosterDialog.svelte) shows the same canvas that gets encoded, scaled down by CSS alone, and that is what makes the character-name switch safe to default to on. A hidden setting deciding whether real names leave the machine would be a trap; a switch under a picture that visibly changes when it moves is a decision the reader actually makes. That switch appears **only on the cast card**, the one card that draws names, because a control that does nothing where it sits is worse than one that comes and goes. The pager is a row of **named chips, not dots**: the deck holds up to four cards, and which is which should be readable before it is clicked. The dialog resolves the first **five** named cast members, walked in ranking order so a deleted character ahead of a kept one costs a slot to nobody. One canvas is redrawn as the reader pages rather than the deck being kept alive, so what gets saved is always the card being looked at. The preview is sized by its **height**: a portrait canvas fitted to the dialog's width runs off the bottom of a phone. Portraits are same-origin, so drawing them leaves the canvas exportable; one that fails to load is simply absent and the card falls back to an initial rather than refusing to draw. Copy-to-clipboard **fails loud** when the browser cannot take an image, since a button that silently does nothing is worse than one that says why.

## Hand-kept couplings

1. **`getUserStats` must stay in the RPC read allowlist** (`READ_METHODS`, server/db.ts) or the bridge refuses it and the panel shows an error with no cause on screen.
2. **The fifteen-minute bucket width is shared** between `QUARTER_HOUR_MS` (server/db.ts) and every consumer that assumes buckets never straddle a local midnight (`derive.ts`, `poster.ts`). Changing it on one side alone silently misfiles days for readers on a half-hour offset and nowhere else, which is the hardest possible bug to notice.
3. **The panel and the cards draw the same three charts** from the same rules: the heatmap (ninetieth-percentile ceiling, four steps, trailing to today), the hour bars (scaled against the busiest hour, quiet hours kept as empty bars, the prime window lit), and the ranked cast bars (every member scaled against the lead). Each is two implementations because one is DOM and one is canvas; a change to a rule belongs in both, or the picture stops matching the screen it came from. The **span** is the one thing that differs by medium and is meant to: a picture cannot be scrolled, so the card takes the most recent year that fits and prints the months it covers underneath, while the panel carries the whole history behind a scrollbar.
4. **Every `measured.*Ms` / `*Tokens` figure has a `*Turns` denominator beside it**, and the panel renders the pair. A new measured figure ships its own count, or the screen loses the one thing that keeps it honest about imported chats.
5. `UserStats` ([`src/lib/types/stats.ts`](../src/lib/types/stats.ts)) is the shape the server returns and the client reads, hand-kept on both sides: the server builds a plain object and nothing type-checks the two against each other. A field added to one is a field to add to the other.
